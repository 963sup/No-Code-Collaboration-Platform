# First Principles

1. State the exact decision, actor, time horizon, success condition, and cost of error.
2. Separate observations, constraints, assumptions, unknowns, value choices, and inherited conventions.
3. Study mature products only for evidence about mechanisms; do not inherit their surface taxonomy automatically.
4. Build the minimum sufficient model: boundary, entities, relationships, states, mechanisms, invariants, ownership, trust boundaries, feedback, and delay.
5. Derive interventions and the do-nothing baseline from the causal model.
6. Compare alternatives by impact, reversibility, information value, cost, risk, and blast radius.
7. Define a falsifiable prediction and the smallest discriminating test that would change the decision.
8. Record what evidence would invalidate the model.

Required output:

- Decision and success condition
- Evidence ledger
- Minimum sufficient model
- Invariants and trust boundaries
- Alternatives and counterfactuals
- Recommended intervention
- Minimum discriminating test
- Remaining unknowns
